# Product Roadmap Rules

## 1. Mandate and intake provenance

Freeze strategy/outcome, portfolio/product/customer scope, as-of/horizon, budget/capacity/risk and authorities. Each feedback/request/bug/idea retains source, verbatim, entity/segment/context/date/rights, duplicate cluster, interpretation and confidence. Volume and seniority are signals, not priority.

## 2. Need and opportunity

Validate job, problem, current alternative and desired outcome. Show qualitative pattern or frequency/reach/severity with denominator/sample/bias where available. Separate customer evidence, market evidence, internal constraint, hypothesis and unknown. An requested feature is not yet a need or requirement.

## 3. Concept and specification

Map opportunity to solution concepts and assumptions. Each concept has target user/job, value hypothesis, in/out scope, acceptance, non-functional requirements, accessibility, data/privacy/security, technical/operating dependencies, support and lifecycle impact. Preserve rejected alternatives and rationale.

## 4. Experiments

Test the riskiest assumption with method, unit/sample/window, baseline, success, kill, harm guardrail, evidence owner, interpretation and next decision. Inconclusive does not mean validated. There is no universal interview count, MVP phase count or release sequence.

## 5. Prioritization and sensitivity

Define criteria, weights, scale/direction, missing-data rule, confidence and normalization before scoring. Include customer outcome, strategic fit, evidence strength, value/economics, effort/capacity, dependency, risk/reversibility and opportunity cost. Run sensitivity and expose rank instability. Critical legal/data/security/customer-harm defects are not averaged away.

## 6. Roadmap and capacity

Use outcome-based horizons and options such as validate, invest, maintain, package, defer, stop or deprecate-review. Show team/skill/budget/environment capacity, WIP, dependencies, sequencing, tradeoffs, trigger and uncertainty. A horizon is not a committed date; a score is not funding authority.

## 7. Portfolio and lifecycle

Assess product/portfolio fit, overlap/cannibalization, interoperability, cost-to-serve, adoption, support, data/IP/security/legal, migration, deprecation and customer continuity. Stop or deprecate proposals require reversible transition evidence and human authority.

## 8. Decision boundary

The Skill prepares decision records; it cannot create tickets, mutate backlog/priority/roadmap, approve funding, start build, commit dates/scope, schedule/release/deprecate products or contact customers. Authorized systems and teams act only after decision, change and release controls.

## 9. D10 pilot

Pilot at least three real contexts: new product concept, material enhancement and maintain/stop/deprecate decision. Compare baseline/v2.3 on duplicate/request-to-need defects, evidence traceability, experiment quality, rank sensitivity, capacity/dependency misses, lifecycle/customer-harm findings, decision quality, token and duration. Static PASS does not prove product-market fit or realized outcome.
