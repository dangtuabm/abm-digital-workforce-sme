# Customer Retention Rules

## 1. Unit and service truth

- Unit is a governed account/contract/service relationship, not a person or device event.
- Freeze as-of, cohort, eligibility, service/contract version, renewal/cancel terms and source snapshots.
- Resolve account-contract-user duplicates; minimize identities and keep authorized purpose.
- Marketing promise, proposal, contract obligation, delivered output and realized customer outcome are distinct classes.

## 2. Lifecycle and outcome contract

Recommended evidence stages: onboarding, activation, adoption, realized value, renewal/exit. Each needs entry, evidence, expected outcome, owner, gap and exit. Do not impose universal day-count or activity threshold; use approved service policy and cohort evidence.

Usage is a signal, not value. Customer outcome needs accepted metric/evidence. Complaint, inactivity, missed event or stakeholder change can support a hypothesis but cannot alone prove churn intent.

## 3. Metric contract

Every metric needs name, purpose, unit/grain, formula, numerator, denominator, cohort, window, source, freshness, missing/late/correction rule, baseline/target source, owner and limitation.

Core categories may include:

- onboarding/activation/adoption;
- outcome realization and time-to-value;
- service quality, incidents, complaints and recovery;
- retention/renewal/churn with explicit eligibility and event date;
- revenue/cost/LTV with currency, period, discount/margin assumptions;
- contact/consent and customer-harm guardrails.

Do not mix logo/revenue/user retention, voluntary/involuntary churn, gross/net retention or observed/predicted LTV.

## 4. Health and risk diagnosis

Health model must be versioned, approved, eligible-unit bounded and calibrated. Each signal has source/confidence; missing is `UNKNOWN`, not zero. Score account/service relationship, never individual worth or persuasion susceptibility.

Risk diagnosis: signal → at least two plausible hypotheses where uncertain → confirming/disconfirming evidence → confidence → next validation. Separate service failure, adoption barrier, fit/value gap, stakeholder/process change, commercial constraint, billing failure and explicit customer choice.

## 5. Intervention contract

Intervention options: support, service recovery, enablement, product/delivery feedback, contract clarification, renewal review, value expansion review, pause or exit review. Each needs affected unit, evidence, customer outcome, consent/channel, owner/capacity, cost/economics, success/stop/guardrail, review and human state.

Expansion is eligible only when realized value/fit and customer need are evidenced. Never hide cancel, add friction, auto-renew/charge without valid terms/notice/authority, spam or use false urgency.

## 6. Cohort and economics reconciliation

Report census, eligible/excluded/active/renewed/churned IDs, reasons, duplicate treatment, period/window, amount/currency, revenue/cost, variance and unresolved items. LTV is a model, not fact: show formula, horizon, margin/discount/churn assumptions, calibration and sensitivity.

## 7. Risks

Minimum groups:

- `SERVICE_DELIVERY_OUTCOME`
- `DATA_METRIC_INTEGRITY`
- `CHURN_CAUSAL_UNCERTAINTY`
- `CUSTOMER_HARM_FAIRNESS`
- `CONSENT_CONTACT_CHOICE`
- `COMMERCIAL_RENEWAL_BILLING`

Critical defects: fabricated outcome/usage/churn/LTV, cohort/denominator manipulation, protected/proxy/vulnerability score, complaint suppression, cancel obstruction, hidden auto-renew/charge, unauthorized contact/offer/discount/renew/upsell/cancel/close.

## 8. D10 pilot

Pilot at least three real contexts: subscription/service renewal, project/managed service and learning/community. Use frozen truth sets; measure entity/metric accuracy, risk precision/recall, intervention decision usefulness, renewal/churn calibration, complaint/cancel compliance, customer outcome/harm, token and duration. Static self-test does not prove retention lift.
